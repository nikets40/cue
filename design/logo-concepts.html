Cue · Identity exploration 01

A mark you can find on a Lock Screen at arm's length.

Four directions for the Cue app pair. Every concept is tested in the places it will actually live: the iOS home screen, the macOS menu bar at 18 pixels, and a Live Activity. The brand world is the theater booth — house lights down, one warm tungsten signal.

1 · The Q-Play

Recommended

Cue is pronounced Q — so the monogram writes itself: a ring with its tail swapped for a play triangle. One shape says both the name and the job. It survives at any size, needs no text, and the tail naturally points “go.”

Primary mark
App icon · stage + tungsten
App icon · reversed
Cue Booth
Menu bar, 15 px template glyph

Why it wins: it is the only direction that stays legible as a one-color 15 px template in the menu bar and reads as a wordless “play” affordance on the Live Activity button cluster.

2 · The Cue Light

In a real theater, the stage manager gives cues with a small signal lamp: standby, then go. A dot with radiating arcs — half beacon, half “broadcasting to your phone.” Warmer and more narrative, slightly less obviously about media.

Primary mark
App icon
Light tile

Risk: at glance-size it can read as a Wi-Fi or AirDrop glyph — the exact system icons it would sit next to in the menu bar.

3 · The C-Play

The literal route: a bold C whose counter holds a play triangle. Friendly, instantly decodes as “media app that starts with C.” The safest of the four — and the one a hundred other media utilities would also pick.

Primary mark
App icon
Reversed

Solid but generic — it describes the category, not this product.

4 · The Cue Card

A stage direction written on a card: rounded rectangle, folded corner, one line of “script” and a position dot — the progress bar as identity. Distinctive and calm, closest to the app's actual UI furniture.

Primary mark
App icon

Ages well, but the fold + line + dot is three ideas; at 15 px it collapses into noise.

Studio recommendation

Ship the Q-Play on the stage-gradient tile, with the tungsten amber #E8A33D as the single brand accent across both apps. Keep the Cue Light as the connection-state motif inside the UI (standby amber → paired green) rather than as the logo — that way the second-best idea still earns its keep. Wordmark: “Cue” set in SF Rounded Semibold beside the mark; Booth uses the same mark, one-color, as its menu-bar template glyph.
